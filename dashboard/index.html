Meta-finding
SELF-REVIEW IS DIRECTIONALLY UNRELIABLE — it catches defects that make the work look WORSE and misses the ones that make it look FINISHED. The two findings that most flattered this cycle's engineering were both false, and neither was caught by self-review: (1) 'deadlock-free by construction' — a safety argument used to justify shipping a new enforcement gate with LIVE TEETH, which held only at the single-agent boundary and failed at the multi-agent one the system actually runs; (2) 'zero external files needed editing' after a governance renumber — a classification the author graded himself and reported as a sweep, which missed three live citations, one of them a self-reference INSIDE the very section renumbered, in the very file swept. Both were caught by an adversarial reviewer. Both became rules. The generalization: a safety claim is scoped to a BOUNDARY, and 'by construction' names the boundary you happened to be looking at — so state the boundary in the claim, and before shipping teeth on it, name the configuration in which it does NOT hold. The cycle then demonstrated the same asymmetry at the ritual layer: the retro declared itself complete with six of ten steps unrun, because 'complete' was stamped on writing the artifact rather than on finishing the work — a finished-looking failure, caught by the operator rather than by the OS.
