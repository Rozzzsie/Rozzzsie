Meta-finding
Every defect this cycle was found either by an internal contradiction between two of my own figures or by a control I built on purpose, and none by an alarm. Nine measurement predicates were wrong, each bounded differently, and every rule that would have caught them was already written down; the discriminator was not knowledge but the moment of checking, since each wrong predicate sat in a verification step written quickly because the subject it checked had already been verified slowly. The cycle's sharpest structural lesson came from the coverage block the last retro criticised for carrying stale counts. Re-deriving three such counts produced a natural experiment. Two disagreed with their published history. The one whose members the prior cycle had written down was adjudicated in a single read, because comparing members rather than counts showed at once which member my matcher had dropped, and the defect turned out to be mine. The one whose members had never been published cannot be adjudicated at all, in either direction, by anyone including its author. A count without its members is not a measurement anybody can inherit. The rule that follows is to publish the members and let the count be derived, and where the list is too large for that, to publish the unit and the matcher so the next cycle can reproduce the figure instead of trusting it.
