A PROVENANCE LABEL IS NOT A VERIFICATION GATE, AND FOUR CORRECT LABELS DID NOT STOP ONE WRONG NUMBER. An unverified count was passed from the orchestrator to the learning-layer agent, which used it, attributed it, and stated explicitly that it could not check it; the figure hardened into three places in that agent's published proposal. It then passed to the consultant rail, which again labelled it correctly and again did not stop it. The number was wrong in three independent ways: wrong magnitude, wrong denominator scope, and a corpus-state mismatch between its two halves with neither half labelled — so the published ratio described a state that never existed. THE LOAD-BEARING ERROR WAS THE DENOMINATOR: a named-rail numerator was paired against an all-dispatch corpus, most of whose members were never owed the thing being counted. That is a join-key error, committed on a figure the author generated himself. Corrective direction: verify BEFORE handing a figure to a deputy; labelling it afterward is a receipt for a check nobody ran.

1.2
A RATIO IS ONE CLAIM, NOT TWO — CORRECTING ONE HALF WHILE TRUSTING THE OTHER MOVED THE PUBLISHED FIGURE FURTHER FROM THE TRUTH THAN LEAVING BOTH ALONE. The learning-layer agent audited a ratio it had been handed, corrected the numerator upward on its own independent census, and flagged the denominator as the orchestrator's and unverified. Both acts were individually correct and locally defensible. The result was worse than inaction, because a one-sided correction transfers the credibility of the checked half onto the unchecked half, and the unverified-flag reads as caution while functioning as a pass. The agent banked this against itself in the proposal rather than quietly fixing the number, which is why it is publishable. Corrective direction: audit a ratio as ONE object — numerator, denominator, and the corpus state of each — or leave it whole and say so.

1.3
A CORRECT RECOMMENDATION CAN REST ON A WRONG PREMISE, AND THE CONCLUSION SURVIVES EVERY CHECK YOU RUN ON IT. A routing recommendation was argued on the grounds that a governance file sat at 94.5 percent of its enforced size bound with roughly four sessions of headroom. Measured, it was at 84.92 percent with 22,614 units of headroom — the premise was ten points off. THE RECOMMENDATION WAS STILL RIGHT, because the true figure sits 0.08 points under the warn threshold, so a substantial addition trips it anyway. This is the hardest shape to catch: agreeing with a conclusion is not evidence about its premise, and every check aimed at the conclusion confirms it. Corrective direction: when you accept a recommendation, state which of its premises you actually verified — accepting the routing and verifying the bound are two different facts.

A VERIFICATION PREDICATE OF PRESENCE WHERE THE RIGHT ONE WAS ASSERTION — AND IT FLAGS THE MOST CAREFUL AUTHORS WHILE PASSING ANYONE WHO OVERWRITES SILENTLY. Checking that a correction had landed, the orchestrator swept for whether the wrong figure was PRESENT and got three hits, and was about to report a failure. All three sat inside the corrected region as CORRECTION RECORDS — contrastive statements of the old value beside the new, which is the house standard. The right predicate was whether the wrong figure was still ASSERTED. 4.1
A PORTABILITY CONSTRAINT DOES NOT FIRE WHEN THE QUESTION PRESENTS AS ACCURACY, AND A DEPUTY INHERITS THE LEAD'S FRAME SILENTLY. A proposal to fix the accuracy of a self-audit instrument was relayed to the operator inside the frame it arrived in. The operator asked a single question — is this for portability — and exposed that the proposal OPTIMISES A MEASUREMENT WITHOUT ASKING WHETHER THE MEASUREMENT BELONGS WHERE IT LIVES. As scoped it would have made the architecture MORE coupled to one specific CLI, against a workspace whose stated value proposition is independence from any one vendor. The governing rule was already live and binding, and its own text nearly predicts the miss: it warns that the constraint fails to fire when a question presents as SECURITY, TOOLING or CONVENIENCE. ADD ACCURACY TO THAT LIST — it is the most disarming presentation of all, because making a number correct reads as strictly-good and frame-neutral, and nobody asks whether a bug fix has an architecture. THE DEPUTY COULD NOT HAVE CAUGHT THIS: a well-executed proposal inside the wrong frame is indistinguishable from a well-executed proposal, so the frame question is the lead's and must be asked BEFORE dispatch.

THE CONSULTANT RAIL RETIRED THE QUESTION IT WAS ASKED RATHER THAN ANSWERING IT, AND THE REFRAME IS THE RESULT. Asked where a governance measurement should be STORED — vendor-specific log versus an artifact the architecture owns — the rail answered that storage is not the axis. The axis is HOW MANY PLACES KNOW HOW TO READ THE RECORD, currently three: a pure module, a bash recipe embedded in a mandated authoring template, and every author's head. The rail also told the orchestrator, correctly, that he had over-corrected: routing an accuracy fix through a portability lens is a category error at the level asked, but the operator's question still earned its keep by RELOCATING the fix rather than reopening it.

Meta-finding
A PROVENANCE LABEL IS NOT A VERIFICATION GATE. An unverified figure was handed to the learning-layer agent, which did everything correctly — it used the figure, attributed it, and stated in writing that it could not check it. The figure still hardened into three separate places in a published proposal. Attribution preserves accountability and does nothing whatsoever to stop propagation: a disclaimed number travels exactly as far as an undisclaimed one and arrives wearing a caveat that reads as diligence. The same figure then passed through a fourth hop, to the consultant rail, which again labelled it correctly and again could not stop it. Four hops, four correct labels, one wrong number. The sharper half is the learning agent's own: A RATIO IS ONE CLAIM, NOT TWO. It audited the pair, corrected the numerator, and flagged the denominator as unverified — and thereby moved the published figure FURTHER from the truth than leaving both alone would have, because the half that was checked lends its credibility to the half that was not. The flag read as caution and functioned as a pass. Corrected, the finding is stronger than either original: not a rate but a DEATH DATE. The observability convention it measures has been dead since a specific day three weeks ago. A rate invites "tighten the discipline"; a date asks what changed on a particular day, which is a question with an answer.
