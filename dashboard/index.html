the weekly hook fire-rate audit hard-FAILED mid-retro — a once-per-session liveness marker emitted by the new memory-tier enforcement primitive's observer (added LAST cycle expressly to make a deliberately-silent hook VISIBLE to the auditor) was written WITHOUT the mandatory schema-version field, and the audit script hard-fails on any in-window record carrying a non-conforming schema version. The standing verify-to-artifact signal, turned on the audit layer itself.

1.2
verify-to-artifact fired INSIDE the retro on the auditor's OWN claim (n+1 cross-cycle, sharpest instance to date) — the orchestrator asserted a gap ('a send-classified gate hook never received its liveness marker') from the producer-LOG alone (a surrogate showing zero records), without reading the gate's source. The learning-layer agent read the source, found the marker present and correctly wired — it only fires when a send-classified tool is actually invoked, so zero records = expected-UNEXERCISED, not absent — and REFUSED to encode the false gap into a rule. The claim was corrected to the source before anything shipped, and became this cycle's headline learning entry. Doctrine: a 'mechanism-absent' claim must be verified at the source artifact, never inferred from a producer-log's silence — log-absence and mechanism-absence are different propositions.

2.1
PROMOTED to doctrine (learning-layer P2) — 'write the liveness/audit marker on the CLEAN path at authoring time, not as a bolt-on.' The audit-break (1.1) and the false-gap (1.2) share a root: a marker that PROVES a hook ran was treated as an afterthought, so it was both schema-malformed AND mis-read. Rule: any hook whose silence is legitimate must emit its liveness marker on the success path at the point of authoring, schema-conformant, so the auditor can distinguish 'ran, no-op by design' from 'never ran.' Verify-to-artifact corollary: the PRESENCE of such a marker is read from the hook SOURCE; its FIRING from the conformant log — never conflate the two.
