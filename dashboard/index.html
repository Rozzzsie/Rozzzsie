Meta-finding
Three of this cycle's findings came from running a producer I had already written instead of the matcher I was about to trust, and the pattern across them is that my own matcher and the real consumer disagreed every single time I checked. Two hand-rolled counts over the improvement backlog returned different totals from each other, and only the producer could say which was right; it was the looser one, which is the opposite of the intuition that a tighter pattern is a safer one. Two sweeps over the commit-boundary hooks returned zero because the validators are invoked through shell variables, so no file name appears on the invocation line at all, and a zero there reads exactly like a clean surface. The cycle's sharpest structural lesson is about a denominator. The ritual's own step text enumerates sixteen labels for the dial that scores it, and the ritual has seventeen; the omitted one is the fold step, which this cycle ran. The dial was not wrong, because the prior cycle had already published seventeen after catching a matcher that dropped a letter-suffixed label — so the published series is correct and the instruction that tells an author how to compute it is the stale surface. That is a failure direction worth naming: a stale instruction beside a correct series will be resolved in the instruction's favour by any author who reads the step and not the history, and the step is what an author reads.
